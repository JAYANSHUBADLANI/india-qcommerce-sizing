## 1. Executive Summary

I conducted a rigorous, data-driven market sizing of the Indian Quick Commerce sector for FY2025-26, triangulating demographic bottom-up models with audited top-down disclosures. 

The Indian Quick Commerce market is estimated at **Rs 128,779 Cr (~$13.5B) in total Gross Order Value (GOV)** for FY26. 

The sector is highly consolidated, with the "Big-3" (Blinkit, Zepto, and Instamart) commanding an estimated 90% market share. Rapid category expansion beyond traditional grocery (into electronics, beauty, and apparel) has structurally altered unit economics, enabling Blinkit to achieve Adjusted EBITDA profitability in Q3 FY26. 

However, my bottom-up demographic analysis reveals severe constraints on future geographic expansion. The Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM) in Tier-2 cities falls dramatically short of the volume required to sustain current dark store unit economics, suggesting the next phase of growth will depend on increasing wallet share in Tier-1 metros rather than greenfield expansion.

## 2. Market Sizing: Top-Down Triangulation

My top-down estimate relies on strict adherence to SEBI-filed financial results and shareholder letters. 

### FY26 Estimated GOV by Player:
*   **Blinkit (Eternal Ltd):** Rs 62,236 Cr (Implied GOV)
    *   *Note on Accounting Shift:* Blinkit transitioned to an inventory-led (1P) model during FY26, reporting Net Order Value (NOV) of Rs 48,595 Cr for the year. I estimated the equivalent GOV using their Q1 FY26 GOV/NOV conversion ratio (1.28x) to maintain comparability.
*   **Instamart (Swiggy Ltd):** Rs 28,496 Cr (Reported GOV)
*   **Zepto:** Rs 25,168 Cr (Estimated GOV)
    *   *Methodology:* Derived from Zepto's June 2026 U-DRHP filing (1,139 stores x 1,677 orders/store/day x 365 days x Rs 361 AOV).

Assuming a 90% combined market share, the implied total market size is **Rs 128,779 Cr ($13.5B)**. This aligns with recent high-end estimates from Bernstein ($11.5B) while exceeding conservative CY2025 projections from Redseer ($7.0B) due to structural outperformance in late FY25 and FY26.

## 3. Market Sizing: Bottom-Up Demographic Funnel

To validate the top-down figures, I constructed a bottom-up demographic funnel starting from the 122.3M urban households in India. 

*   **Addressable Base:** Filtering for income (>Rs 30k/month) and digital readiness (smartphone + UPI), I identified a core addressable market of **31.3M households**.
*   **Adoption & Frequency:** Calibrating against Blinkit's disclosed 27.2M monthly transacting customers (Q4 FY26), I estimate industry-wide penetration of ~13.5M unique households, ordering an average of 8 times per month across all platforms in Tier-1 cities.
*   **Calculated TAM:** This bottom-up approach yields a TAM of **Rs 68,599 Cr ($7.2B)**.

**Reconciliation Gap:** The bottom-up TAM is 46.7% lower than the top-down reality. Sensitivity analysis indicates this gap is driven by (a) underestimating the frequency of multi-platform usage, (b) the rise of non-household institutional ordering (offices, cafes), and (c) the accelerating AOV premium of non-grocery categories (electronics).

## 4. Competitive Dynamics & Positioning

The FY25 GOV-based market share (the last full year of clean, pre-1P comparable data for all three players) demonstrates Blinkit's clear leadership:

1.  **Blinkit (~44.5% share):** The dominant market leader, reaching Adjusted EBITDA profitability (+Rs 37 Cr) by Q4 FY26. Its scale (2,243 stores, 915M annual orders) and dense Delhi-NCR footprint provide a massive fixed-cost advantage.
2.  **Zepto (~32.4% share):** The fastest-growing pure-play operator. While still operating at an EBITDA loss (Rs 78.75/order in FY26), Zepto achieves the highest capital efficiency per store (up to 2,140 orders/day in peak quarters) and commands strong youth demographic loyalty.
3.  **Instamart (~23.1% share):** Swiggy's quick commerce arm is shifting strategy toward "Megapod" stores (10,000+ sq ft) to drive larger basket sizes, reaching contribution break-even in May 2026.

### The Grocery vs. Non-Grocery Shift
I note explicitly that this is no longer just a "grocery delivery" market. While precise category splits are not cleanly disclosed, the rising AOV and improving margin profiles are heavily subsidized by the expansion into electronics, beauty, and apparel, changing the fundamental nature of the sector.

## 5. Strategic Conclusion: The Tier-2 Expansion Trap

My Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM) analysis reveals a critical constraint for new entrants or players seeking greenfield growth in Tier-2 cities.

*   **The Economics:** Blinkit's benchmark requires ~Rs 7.0 lakh in daily GOV for a dark store to reach contribution breakeven. 
*   **The Tier-2 Reality:** For a well-funded new entrant attempting to capture 8% market share across the top 25 Tier-2 cities with 180 dark stores, the required Total Addressable Market in those cities would need to be **Rs 57,488 Cr**. 
*   **The Gap:** My demographic model estimates the actual SAM in those 25 cities is only **Rs 3,415 Cr**, a 94% shortfall against the breakeven threshold.

**Recommendation:** Quick commerce remains an inherently dense, Tier-1 phenomenon. Capital deployment should prioritize SKU expansion (electronics, apparel), store format innovation (e.g., Instamart's Megapods), and wallet-share capture within the top 8-10 metros, rather than pursuing unprofitable geographic expansion into Tier-2 markets where demand density cannot support the infrastructure.
